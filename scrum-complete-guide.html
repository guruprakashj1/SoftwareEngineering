1. Scrum Fundamentals

What is Scrum?

Scrum is an agile framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products. It is designed to help teams work together effectively through iterative and incremental development.

Core Principles

  • Transparency: All aspects of the process must be visible to those responsible for the outcome
  • Inspection: Regular checks of progress toward sprint goals
  • Adaptation: Adjusting quickly to changes

Values

  • Commitment: Team commits to achieving goals together
  • Courage: Face challenges and hard problems
  • Focus: Concentrate on sprint work and team goals
  • Openness: Be transparent about work and challenges
  • Respect: Respect team members' capabilities and independence

  • Stakeholder management

  • Delivers product increments

  • Cross-functional

  • Testing
  • Technical decisions

3. Scrum Artifacts

Product Backlog

  • Ordered list of everything needed in the product
  • Single source of requirements
  • Dynamic and constantly evolving
  • Managed by Product Owner

Sprint Backlog

  • Selected Product Backlog items for Sprint
  • Plan for delivering increment
  • Owned by Development Team
  • Updated throughout Sprint

Increment

  • Sum of all completed backlog items
  • Must be "Done" as per definition
  • Must be usable
  • Must meet team's quality criteria

4. Scrum Ceremonies

Ceremony Duration Purpose Participants
Sprint Planning 8 hours max for 1-month Sprint Plan the work for the Sprint Entire Scrum Team
Daily Scrum 15 minutes Sync up on progress and obstacles Development Team
Sprint Review 4 hours max for 1-month Sprint Review increment and adapt backlog Scrum Team and stakeholders
Sprint Retrospective 3 hours max for 1-month Sprint Process improvement Scrum Team

5. Scrum Metrics

Velocity

  • Average amount of work completed per Sprint
  • Used for planning and forecasting
  • Measured in story points or hours

Sprint Burndown

  • Tracks remaining work in Sprint
  • Updated daily
  • Shows progress toward Sprint Goal

Release Burndown

  • Tracks progress toward release
  • Shows remaining work over Sprints
  • Helps forecast completion dates

6. Best Practices and Tips

For Successful Sprints

  • Keep Sprints timeboxed and consistent
  • Maintain clear Definition of Done
  • Protect team from interruptions
  • Ensure daily communication
  • Regular backlog refinement

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Overcommitting in Sprint Planning
  • Skipping ceremonies
  • Poor backlog management
  • Lack of team empowerment
  • Insufficient stakeholder engagement
